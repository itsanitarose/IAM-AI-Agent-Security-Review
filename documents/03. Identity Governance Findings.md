# 03. Identity Governance Findings

## 1. Looking Beyond Permissions

After reviewing SupportBot's permissions, I turned to how its identity was being managed.

The configuration raised a bigger question for me:

**If something goes wrong with this AI agent, who is accountable, how far could the damage spread, and what would stop it?**

## 2. Risk Rating Criteria

I considered two questions:

**Impact:** What's the worst thing that could reasonably happen?

**Exposure:** How easy would it be to misuse the access, and is there any sign it may already have been misused?

| Severity        | What It Means                                                                                                                                                      |
| :-------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 **Critical** | Serious potential harm, such as large amounts of patient data being exposed, unauthorised payments or evidence being erased, with a direct opportunity for misuse. |
| 🟠 **High**     | Significant potential impact, or a weakness that allows other risks to continue or spread.                                                                         |
| 🟡 **Medium**   | More limited impact, or existing controls that reduce the risk.                                                                                                    |
| 🟢 **Low**      | A minor security weakness with little direct risk.                                                                                                                 |

*These are qualitative ratings for this fictional scenario, not calculated risk scores.*

## 3. Governance Findings

| Finding                                                  | Severity                | Risk                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| :------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **No recorded identity owner**                           | High                    | SupportBot was created by a contractor who has since left, and its account has no named owner. If something goes wrong, it's unclear who is responsible for reviewing its access, managing its credentials or deciding whether to disable it. This could delay important security decisions.                                                      |
| **Non-expiring, unrotated API token**                    | High                    | If someone steals or copies the token, they might be able to use the system as if they were SupportBot. Because the token never expires, that access could continue until the credential is revoked.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Shared credential across environments**                | High                    | If an attacker obtains the token from staging, they could potentially use it against production too. If production accepts it, SupportBot's broad permissions could expose live patient information or allow unauthorised financial actions.                                                                                                      |
| **No periodic identity and access review**               | High                    | SupportBot's access hasn't been reviewed since launch. Unnecessary permissions could remain active for months, including the ability to issue refunds even though that isn't part of the agent's job.                                                                                                                                             |
| **Refund authority outside documented responsibilities** | High                    | SupportBot can issue refunds up to $500 without human approval. Two refunds, worth $120 and $310, were recorded without approval. This shows the permission has been used, but doesn't prove either refund was incorrect or fraudulent.                                                                                                           |
| **Excessive access to sensitive information**            | **Critical in context** | SupportBot can read full patient records despite only needing limited information for support questions. If its identity is misused, large amounts of patient data could be exposed. Tuesday's unusual bulk access makes this risk more urgent, but doesn't prove that its token was stolen or that data was disclosed to an unauthorised person. |

To check my understanding of the credential risks, I referred to the [OWASP Secrets Management Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secrets_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html), focusing on token expiration, rotation and revocation.

## 4. Governance Risk Summary

What concerns me most is **how these risks reinforce each other**.

SupportBot has no recorded owner, excessive permissions and an API token that never expires. Together, these weaknesses create a **toxic combination**.

If someone steals or copies SupportBot's API token, they might be able to use the system as if they were SupportBot. Its excessive permissions could then allow access to sensitive patient information or unauthorised financial actions.

Because staging and production share the credential, exposure in one environment could also put the other at risk wherever the token is accepted.

Without a clearly assigned owner and regular access reviews, these weaknesses could remain unresolved.

**My assessment:** This is more than excessive access. It's an identity governance problem where missing accountability and weak credential management could increase the damage if SupportBot's identity is misused.

Tuesday's unusual activity makes these concerns more urgent, but **does not confirm that the token was stolen or misused**.

Before deciding on long-term security controls, I would prioritise understanding what happened, assessing the risk to patient information and identifying the immediate actions needed to contain a possible compromise.
