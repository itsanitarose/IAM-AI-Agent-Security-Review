# 02. Permission Review

## 1. Reviewing SupportBot's Access

SupportBot's job is to answer appointment and billing questions from clinic staff, create support tickets and draft responses for human approval.

I reviewed its nine permissions against those responsibilities and the activity recorded last week, asking:

**Does the agent need this access to do its job, or does it give SupportBot more authority than it should have?**

## 2. Permission Assessment

| Current Permission | Decision | My Reasoning |
|:-------------------|:---------|:-------------|
| Read help-centre articles | **Needed** (keep) | SupportBot needs approved information to answer routine questions. |
| Read all patient records (full ePHI) | **Needed with conditions** (replace broad access) | Full patient records aren't necessary. Allow only relevant appointment or patient-status information. The 38,400 records accessed last week show how extensively this permission is used and why its scope matters. |
| Read billing summaries | **Needed with conditions** (restrict) | Allow only billing information for patients the requesting clinic staff member is authorised to access. |
| Read full billing and payment data | **Not needed** (remove) | Billing summaries are sufficient for routine questions. Sensitive payment details aren't needed. |
| Update patient contact details | **Not needed** (remove) | Three updates occurred last week. I would check whether they were authorised and route future changes through approved staff. |
| Issue refunds up to $500 without approval | **Not needed** (remove) | Two refunds ($120 and $310) were issued without recorded human approval. Finance should approve and issue refunds. |
| Send email as `support@northwindhealth.com` | **Not needed** (remove) | SupportBot sent 612 emails last week, although its documented role is to draft responses for human review and sending. |
| Create and update support tickets | **Needed with conditions** (restrict) | Allow ticket creation and necessary updates related to authorised requests, without access to unrelated tickets or sensitive fields. |
| Delete ticket history | **Not needed** (remove) | Ticket history must remain available for accountability. No deletions were recorded last week, but the permission is still unnecessary for the documented role. |

## 3. An Important Distinction: Access to Patient Data

One permission needed a closer look.

SupportBot may need appointment information to answer a clinic staff member's question. But **that doesn't mean it needs unrestricted access to every patient's full medical record**.

I would replace the broad ePHI permission with a narrower way to retrieve only the information needed for the current request.

The application should check the staff member's identity, clinic and authorised patient access before returning information. **SupportBot should not be able to retrieve more patient information than the requesting staff member is allowed to see.**

I wanted to understand how these restrictions could actually be enforced, so I looked into the [OWASP AI Agent Security Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html).

What stood out to me was that **authorisation should be enforced outside the agent**, rather than trusting it to decide its own access limits.

Applying that principle to SupportBot, I would recommend that the application check which clinic and patient records the requesting staff member is authorised to access before returning any information to the agent. SupportBot should then receive only the fields needed to answer that specific question.
## 4. My Assessment

I would:

- **Remove five permissions:** Full billing and payment data, patient contact updates, issuing refunds, sending emails and deleting ticket history.
- **Replace one broad permission:** Full patient record access with narrowly scoped appointment or patient status access.
- **Restrict two permissions:** Billing summaries and support-ticket updates.
- **Keep one permission:** Reading approved help centre articles.

My aim is to let SupportBot continue helping clinic staff **without giving it unnecessary access to sensitive information or authority to issue refunds, change patient details or erase records**.

These are proposed changes. The backend application/API would need to enforce access restrictions independently of SupportBot. Basically, **SupportBot requests information, and the backend application/API checks whether access is allowed.**

I would also recommend reviewing recent activity to determine whether any actions were unauthorised.
